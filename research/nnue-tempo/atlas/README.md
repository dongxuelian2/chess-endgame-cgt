# Which endgame distinctions survive a classical tie?

We enumerated 214,063,938 distinct nonterminal, nonchecked boards in 25 four-man material classes. Every board has a Syzygy label, two classical scores and the three native SF14.1 NNUE readouts. The complete one-reply analysis uses 107,031,969 horizontal-reflection representatives and all 1,543,690,301 of their legal replies. Forty-four of 48 proposed chess rules erase a strict WDL distinction between classically tied moves somewhere in this domain. Twenty-seven admit a witness where exactly one of the 48 restrictions erases that particular distinction. The [chess findings ledger](FINDINGS.md) describes each rule, its evidence and the sparse or empty cases.

The [quiet endings and two-coordinate head study](horizon/README.md) follows the unresolved quiet comparisons with new played positions, bounded conversion proofs and exhaustive knight- and bishop-versus-pawn interventions.

## What the original hand evaluation loses

White and Black each have one king and one P, N, B, R or Q. Fix the two kings, the black man's square and both piece types. Vary the white man's square, keeping Black to move. Partition the resulting boards by Stockfish 14.1's full classical score before final grain truncation. We disable both classical lazy cutoffs and preserve the native specialized ending functions. Take optimal play after the static position from the sign of Syzygy WDL. Cursed wins and blessed losses use the sign of the five-valued WDL code. The corpus uses empty castling and en-passant rights, no position history, and a zero clock. Board legality is checked locally. A material class identifies the colors of the two men.

Given the fixed context and classical scalar, any function predicting WDL must make at least the size of each score class minus its largest WDL count in errors. A modal-label lookup attains this bound. Summation over the complete census gives **13,265,530 unavoidable errors**. Adding the integer neural score to the information given to this lookup reduces the empirical minimum to **2,673,035**. This is the finite-domain capacity of a lookup with access to the context. The independent ordering test pairs different WDL labels of equal full classical score within that context. SF14.1 orders 69.65% of 342,175,835 such pairs correctly with half credit for ties. Pairs here can lack a common legal source. The move tests below require one. [Exact counts and numerators](results/census.csv)

Several classical formulas give an exact reason for the collision. In KRvKB the value depends on the defending king’s proximity to the edge. In KRvKN the additional input is the distance between the defending king and knight. Both ignore the attacking rook square. In KQvKR the material term, distance between the kings and defending king's edge score ignore both nonking placements. KQvKP uses the two kings and the pawn, including the supported seventh-rank fortress test on selected files, and omits the queen square. For example, in KBvKR the equal-score comparisons whose WDL order requires rook captures include 9,348,460 common-source bishop move pairs. SF14.1 gets 62.2% correct. Swapping the material colors changes the task because Black always has the reply. Horizontal reflection preserves the legality mask and WDL of every pair. All 107,031,969 mirrored pairs have identical native neural scores. The released classical score differs in 2,201,304 and the full pre-grain classical score in 6,261,134. Consequently the move census explicitly takes the representative with White's king on files a–d. ## A sign reversal that requires joint piece geometry

Fix both kings, material, the white starting square and the same two quiet legal moves to squares *i* and *j*. Place the black nonking piece on *y* or *z*. The four resulting boards form a rectangle. Require equal classical score and exactly the same 48 reply-availability flags across *i* and *j* within each row. Syzygy must strictly prefer opposite destinations in the two rows. Each rectangle contributes once over all shared legal white sources. The initial [19 rook-versus-pawn successes](../pawn-geometry/README.md) motivated this test. The new geometry covers all 25 material classes and also controls all 48 legal-reply indicators.

With kings and piece types fixed, a separable score has the form $S(x,y)=f(x)+g(y)$. The margin $S(i,y)-S(j,y)=f(i)-f(j)$ has a fixed sign for every enemy square. Any fixed monotone output quantizer preserves this obstruction to two strictly correct opposite preferences. More generally, adding any function of the classical scalar and 48 flags to the score leaves that margin unchanged whenever those observables agree within each row. In the strongest subset all four classical scores and signatures coincide. There one can even allow the functions $f$ and $g$ to depend on the common signature and classical score. The same argument applies. A strictly correct reversal certifies a dependence of the ranking on both pieces' placements with the kings and material fixed. There is also a uniform approximation bound. Give win/draw/loss the values +1/0/−1. For truth $V$ form the mixed difference $D=V(i,y)-V(j,y)-V(i,z)+V(j,z)$. The two strict opposite choices give $|D|\ge 2$. Every separable $S$ has mixed difference zero. The triangle inequality therefore gives $\max |S-V|\ge |D|/4\ge 1/2$ over those four boards.

The complete census contains 5,123,091 informative same-source pairs with tied classical score and identical flags. They form 1,313,752 distinct crossover rectangles in 12 material classes. SF14.1 strictly recovers both preferences on 663,639. Its learned direct piece-square branch recovers both on zero, as predicted, and its nonlinear positional head alone recovers both on 658,097. Under equality of the classical score and flags on **all four boards**, 250,321 rectangles survive in ten material classes. Intact NNUE gets both directions right in 141,837 (56.66%), the nonlinear head alone in 143,951. The unit of analysis here is a configuration of four boards. A board can participate in multiple rectangles. These measurements identify an input interaction and an output path. They leave the detailed algorithm inside the nonlinear head open. ## From a chess observation to a causal game test

Choose two destinations for a quiet, nonchecking, nonpromoting move by the same white man. Both moves must be available at the same parent. For each fixed background, each destination pair contributes once however many sources are possible. Require different true outcomes and identical full classical scores. Each Black child has at least one legal reply. Exclude en-passant-creating double moves. We test what changes if Black temporarily loses access to precisely the legal replies with one specified property, and optimal orthodox chess resumes after that reply. If a child has no remaining move we count compulsion separately. All 48 predicates are executable and use ordinary move generation, board geometry and, for recapture or mate, White's immediate legal replies. [Definitions](rules.def)

For each reply class we obtain three different observations. It can be available. It can be necessary to achieve the original WDL in a particular child. Finally it can be necessary for the *difference between* two original WDL labels. Each statement has its own denominator. Of the 48 classes, 46 have a position where suppressing the class lowers Black's result and an alternative reply exists. Of the 44 with an erased move distinction, 40 cover at least 1,000 pairs. SF14.1 beats half credit in 38 of those 40 classes. The pair sets for a clean capture and any nonking capture have Jaccard similarity 0.940. Quiet promotion and a promotion surviving the immediate reply have similarity 0.899. Two classes can overlap strongly and still have very different sufficient or necessary examples. [Overlap counts](results/complete-overlap.csv) · [Exclusive witnesses with legal moves](results/exclusive-certificates.json)

Consider White Kb6 and Rd7 against Black Ka8 and Ra1, White to move. After Rb7, ...Rb1+ skewers the king against the rook and wins. After Rd2, ...Rb1+ is still the only drawing reply. Its geometric role has changed. The classical scores are both zero. The raw neural scores, from Black's perspective, are +11 and −29. Deleting just skewering replies gives draw/draw. ### Controlling the number of deleted moves

Let a child have M legal replies. Removing a tagged class leaves k replies and N(s) original replies have WDL at most s from Black's perspective. Under uniform retention of k replies, the probability that the new best value is at most s is the binomial coefficient C(N(s),k) divided by C(M,k). For the originally higher-valued child H and lower-valued child L, independently choose deletion sets of the observed sizes. The exact probability the original ordering disappears is the sum over s of F_H(s) times P_L(s). This includes both a tie and reversal. We compute this probability for each eligible pair, then sum. The control conditions on which moves are present, the two deletion counts and the original WDL distribution in both children. It also captures how easy a distinction is to erase by arbitrary deletion. [Implementation](deletion.py) · [All observed and expected counts](results/size-matched-deletions.csv)

In the independent replication sample, deleting skewers erased 3,121 distinctions against 199.2 expected from size alone. Deleting absolute pins erased 1,415 against 117.4, and deleting checking double attacks erased 3,643 against 1,604.3. Underpromotions give a useful opposite result. With underpromotion deletion, three distinctions vanished against 870.1 expected under equal-sized random deletion. Underpromotion matters in particular exact positions and is usually replaceable by another promotion in this selected panel. Direct king interposition into an enemy sliding attack has zero legal examples, as king safety predicts. The full corpus also contains restrictions that affect individual pawn positions while failing to erase a same-man classical tie. [Ledger](FINDINGS.md)

## Which part of the network carries each distinction?

SF18's large network has explicit FullThreats inputs alongside ordinary position features. The four disjoint sets of emitted directed features comprise attacks by a black man on the white man, attacks in the opposite direction, hostile contact involving a king, and friendly protection. The encoder omits certain attacker–target combinations and canonically represents certain symmetric relations. The experimental classes refer to edges actually emitted at the *static board before Black moves*. We retain each of the 16 possible subsets, reset the accumulators, run the unchanged native large network, and read the sum of its two raw outputs. Both endpoints are independently checked against the original trace and previous global knockout. The released evaluator's network selection, weighting and scaling receive a separate control. [SF18 encoder](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/blob/cb3d4ee9b47d0c5aae855b12379378ea1439675c/src/nnue/features/full_threats.cpp) · [Release-policy comparison](results/release-policy.csv)

For each pair the intervention utility is correct ordering with half credit for a tie. Average each group's marginal utility over the 24 possible insertion orders. The four exact Shapley contributions sum to the intact-minus-ablated behavioral change on each pair. These are effects of fixed feature interventions in the trained network. A group can hurt decision accuracy in a particular context. Different chess tests can select some of the same pairs. [Factorial results](results/channel-interventions.csv)

On 82,512 replication comparisons, disabling all explicit threat edges reduced raw SF18 accuracy from 73.41% to 59.19%. The four allocations in the order above are +2.02, +7.82, +5.85 and −1.47 percentage points. The total difference was +17.88 points in the 1,647 informative actual-game comparisons. Separately, SF18's *released static evaluator* achieved 65.07% and 86.16% on these two panels. The raw large network is the primary object of the channel intervention. The distinction matters even for a fixed set of leaf positions.

The tag-specific intervals use a cluster multiplier maximum over eligible tags in each panel, treating symmetry groups of root king placements as clusters. We include tags with at least 30 observations, at least ten clusters and nonzero estimated variability. Twenty tags have a positive simultaneous 95% lower bound for the total explicit-channel gain on the replication panel. These descriptive intervals use the finite experimental sampling scheme. ## Can a transparent rule repair the choice?

We orient each pair so the higher score favors Black. For each child compute a binary signature of *available* legal reply classes. Fit one L2-regularized logistic coefficient per class on 62,387 discovery contexts with the unregularized intercept fixed at zero and penalty 0.01. One deterministic, uniformly hashed informative pair represents each fixed context. The fitted rule sums the coefficients for the available replies. A simpler rule awards a point for each of king capture, nonking capture or a surviving promotion. Neither predictor receives restricted WDL labels as input. Computing these rules uses legal reply generation, including immediate checks for recapture or mate. NNUE reads its static input. Equal evaluation cost is an open engineering question.

In the disjoint replication sample the simple rule scores 87.06%, the 48-weight rule 93.43%, SF12 raw NNUE 57.80%, and SF14.1 raw NNUE 67.26%. On the actual games the corresponding numbers are 87.74%, 90.74%, 70.89% and 89.19%. The paired game interval for fitted minus SF14.1 spans −0.78 to +3.71 percentage points. The fit improves on the three-feature rule on both panels. The sample membership conditions specify a strict WDL difference and a classical tie. [Paired intervals](results/paired-effects.csv) · [Frozen coefficients](results/coefficients.csv)

Removing easy immediate tactics gives a different ordering. Among 276 played comparisons in which neither child offers Black a capture or promotion, the fitted rule scores 63.41% and SF14.1 reaches 95.29%. On 18,483 newly generated examples in that stratum the corresponding accuracies are 73.56% and 77.80%. There are also 30,982 distinct replication contexts, before the one-eighth subsample for SF18, where both children have exactly the same 48-bit signature and different WDL. Any deterministic function of the signature, classical score and fixed context ties the two in that pair. SF14.1 earns 24,759 points, or 79.91%. These residuals give concrete targets for deeper explanations. [Quiet residuals](results/quiet-residual.csv) · [Signature collisions](results/rule-collisions.csv)

### Full sets of candidate moves

At each eligible White parent keep *every* quiet, nonchecking, nonpromoting move of the same nonking piece leading to a valid sampled child with no legal en passant. First retain the moves tied for best full classical score. Then apply a secondary score and break remaining ties uniformly. Treat a White win as +1, a draw as 0 and a loss as −1. The exhaustive single-rule tests orient each rule by discovery pair directions. A predictor that rewards the *availability* of a move can have a different effect from deleting that move from the game. The discovered sign and multivariate coefficient are recorded separately in the coefficient table.

There are 88,888,274 distinct candidate parents with at least two choices, 63,467,199 with multiple classical best choices and 16,780,539 where the best choices have different WDL. On the latter parents the mean increase in expected WDL over the original classical tie is +0.348 for the fitted evaluator, +0.287 for the simple rule and +0.181 for SF14.1. The maximum available from perfect tie refinement is +0.428. The corresponding increases over *all* parents are +0.0657, +0.0541 and +0.0341. Of 48 single rules, 35 had positive mean gain in this finite population and 24 had positive mean gain both here and on the played parents. The domain fixes the material, player to move, classical-state convention and eligible alternatives. The results quantify representation loss, static ranking, one-reply game causality, and behavior under specified internal edge removals. Chess-game strength with adaptive search, additional material and more distant tactical consequences remain separate experimental questions. ## Reproduction

Prepare the pinned SF12, SF14.1 and SF18 sources, weights, existing native trace binaries and python-chess/Syzygy data with the [base setup script](../setup.sh). Run the command below from the repository root. It downloads the six verified game archives and builds isolated instrumented binaries in scratch. The runner retains dense tables there for reanalysis. A Linux x86-64 AVX2 host, C++ compiler, make, curl, zstd and Python 3.13+ with NumPy are required.

~~~bash
bash research/nnue-tempo/atlas/reproduce.sh .scratch/nnue
~~~
