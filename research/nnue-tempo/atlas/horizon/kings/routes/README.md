# From king-relative inputs to endgame decisions

In Stockfish 14.1 the rook-to-queen substitution changes the operating range of the first hidden layer. In a third-rank pawn escort the black-king view supplies much of that change. Advance the very same pawn to the second rank and the white-king view can open a different route, even with the rook still on the board. Five identifiable input channels connect the pawn's square, the defender's access to promotion, and the final score. Rook skewers and knight forks give separate tests at both dense layers.

All results use the released network, its native integer head, and the sign of fresh-clock Syzygy WDL. H and L denote two moves by the same white man from a common legal parent, with the better Black outcome on H. The full hand evaluator ties the children unless the experiment explicitly changes the filter. An ordering tie earns half a point. A king–king–black-piece placement can support many different sibling pairs. [Baseline and chess interventions](../README.md) · [Released HalfKAv2_hm input indices](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/blob/7262fd5d14810b7b495b5038e348a448fda1bcc3/src/nnue/features/half_ka_v2_hm.cpp)

## Why the rook and queen use different coordinates

The original 2,830 matched escort comparisons use 23 placements, 562 distinct child geometries, and black pawns on c3 or f3 in the horizontal representative. In 2,385 pairs only the better White move L places the piece on the pawn file or the last rank. The other 445 still show the routing difference. For example White Kd4, Re6 against Kh1, Pf3 can choose Re7 or Rf6. Unit 2 goes from 0 on e7 to 30 on f6, and the Black scores are +93 and −158. Substitute Qe6 and unit 2 stays at zero on both children. Units 4, 6 and 7 change from (0, 0, 126) to (59, 31, 67). The scores become −44 and −698. The table also includes an example in which the two rook children have identical first-hidden bytes. [Chess geometry counts](results/destinations.csv) · [FENs and native activations](results/escort-example.csv)

The accumulator has 1,024 coordinates per king perspective. FT indices 0–1023 are the Black view and 1024–2047 the White view in these Black-to-move positions. Each raw coordinate is its bias plus four king-bucket-conditioned piece-square weights. Clipping each sum into [0,127] gives the input to eight H1 units. Each H1 and each of the 32 H2 units uses an affine transformation, a six-bit right shift and clipping. A direct piece-square term also reaches the output. Unit numbers are zero-based within each layer.

| On the original escort pairs | Mean absolute H1 gap, unit 2 | Unit 6 | Unit 7 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Rook inputs | 29.60 | 1.36 | 0.16 |
| Exchange just the Black-view rook inputs for the queen inputs | 1.29 | 28.36 | 72.94 |
| Exchange just the White view | 27.14 | 7.31 | 0.00 |
| Queen inputs in both views | 2.28 | 30.26 | 58.26 |

These gaps measure how far each byte moves between the two candidate squares. There are two components to a piece substitution at fixed kings. One is the rounded mean difference between queen and rook input columns over all 64 squares in that king bucket. The remainder varies with the actual square. Substituting just the mean difference in both views moves the unit-7 gap from 0.16 to 36.71. Substituting just the square-dependent remainder gives 0.45. Both give 58.26 after the nonlinearities. At H1, independently exchanging the midpoint and difference of the two preactivations gives an exact two-factor allocation of the 58.10-byte increase. The midpoint accounts for 51.63 bytes and the changed displacement for 6.47. The midpoint allocation similarly accounts for −21.57 of the −27.33-byte change in unit 2. On the rook, both unit-7 endpoints clip to zero in 98.30% of pairs. On the queen the figure is 1.73%. In the reverse direction unit 2 clips to zero at both ends in 13.07% and 87.14% respectively. [Input exchanges](results/input-exchange.csv) · [Midpoint and displacement exchanges](results/gate-exchange.csv) · [All eight units](results/hidden-units.csv)

Three Black-view coordinates show how a material change can set the operating range. Across all 562 seed geometries FT333 is zero on the rook and positive on the queen. FT625 is positive on the rook and zero on the queen. FT961 changes from exactly 0 to exactly 127 on every board. A constant across both moves can thus alter how a later neuron responds to a move. FT625 has outgoing signed weights +39, −29 and −41 to H1 units 2, 6 and 7. Transplant just those three FT bytes from queen into rook, keeping every other byte and the rook's direct branch. Accuracy with only units 6/7 differing rises from 53.00% to 75.76%, and accuracy with only unit 2 differing falls from 91.24% to 49.81%. The reciprocal transplant takes the queen's 6/7 result from 87.78% to 73.19%. Individual transplants can have different signs from the combination. The ledger includes 24 fixed random triples. [Input weights and contributions](results/feature-columns.csv) · [Three-channel interventions](results/channel-interchange.csv)

At H2, the mean signed score difference allocated to the rook's 2→11 and 2→14 edges is +119.65 and +81.94 native units. On the queen the 7→30 and 7→26 edges carry +56.09 and +47.58. These are exact endpoint accounting terms. For H2 unit j, multiply each incoming change by the secant of its observed quantizer and its outgoing weight. Summing the edges recovers the positional score difference. Opposing edges can cancel. Separate donor interventions test the change in the actual ordering when an H2 coordinate loses its native difference. ## How far does the escort generalize?

Across black pawn ranks 2–7, exactly 5,834 quiet pairs have a common legal source and different Syzygy outcomes for the two moves, with each move retaining its outcome when the rook becomes a queen. The classical tie filter yields exactly the same set as dropping it. Of these 5,834, 72 have a second-rank pawn, 5,651 a third-rank pawn and 111 a fourth-rank pawn. Requiring the loss of the strict order upon removing the Black escort in both materials leaves exactly the original 2,830 third-rank pairs. The escort edit removes Black king moves guarding the next pawn square on Black's second turn after an initial push. All other nodes retain ordinary play, including a node where a restriction would erase every legal choice. The earlier FT switches also change with the opponent. ## Five input channels respond to a pawn with an exposed promotion square

Move the seed pawn from the third rank to the second while preserving both original choices, both kings and the moving piece. There are 2,487 surviving move pairs. On the rook, unit 7's mean absolute gap changes from **0.17 to 104.64** and unit 2's gap falls to zero. Swapping just the White king's input half gives 86.41 for unit 7, swapping just the Black half gives 5.59. The second-rank experiment admits immediate promotion. Its board labels include 2,166 original-order and 321 equal-WDL rook comparisons. On the queen, 2,460 of the same 2,487 comparisons have become equal WDL. A large activation change and a change of game outcome are distinct measurements. [Physical relocations](results/pawn-relocations.csv) · [Rank-specific interventions](results/rank-shift.csv)

There is a useful exact constraint on this explanation. With both kings fixed, write a raw accumulator as B + P(rank) + M(square). Taking the difference between the two white destinations cancels P(rank) identically. Therefore the mixed move-by-rank difference before the first clipping is exactly zero. We verified this identity coordinate by coordinate on every retained relocation. The change in move sensitivity appears when the shifted sum crosses a clipping threshold and when later units encounter a different operating range. [Rank-dependent input contributions](results/rank-columns.csv)

Orient the board from the white king's viewpoint, reflecting it horizontally when needed to put that king on files e–h. Of the 1,024 White-view input channels, 94 have the same unique highest black-pawn weight over all 48 legal pawn squares in every one of the 32 king buckets. They cover 39 oriented squares. We followed five channels for which the target is on rank two, the on-ray weight suppression has the same sign in all 32 buckets, and the mean byte falls by more than 32 between off-ray and clear-ray defenders in each of the four defender materials. This is a selection using the enumerated data. Both channels also read the defender. At c2, FT1424's mean byte is 100.69 when a white rook is off the promotion ray, 2.14 when its ray to c1 would be clear after the pawn advances, and 123.49 when a king blocks that ray. For a white queen the three means are 92.90, 7.09 and 107.19. The coverage test removes the pawn from its current square and checks the defender's line to promotion. The ledger counts occupation of the promotion square separately. Holding the other pieces and the pawn fixed, each of 641 eligible one-square white-king edits that opens the rook's ray lowers FT1424, by 124.32 bytes on average. On 217 parent boards permitting both opening and still-blocked king edits, the mean contrast is −124.59. Exchange just FT1424 and FT1196 between the original third-rank rook board and its second-rank counterpart. For the 1,849 comparisons with a pawn moved to oriented c2, this raises the H1 unit-7 gap from 0.18 to 71.00, against the natural second-rank gap of 102.30. Freezing both channels at their third-rank values on the second-rank board brings the gap down to 14.34. On the 638 f2 comparisons the corresponding gaps are 0.14, 102.53, 111.41 and 9.81. The chief FT1424→H1 weights include −90 to unit 2 and +99 to unit 7. [Individual and joint transplants](results/pawn-channel-exchange.csv)

**Does the final evaluation use the five channels?** Allow immediate promotions and hash-select a common-source, strict-WDL pair with equal classical scores per material and placement. The four material panels yield 35,969 second-rank comparisons. Select the five oriented files and require that suppressing every legal Black promotion on its first turn erases the ordering. This leaves 17,516 pairs. On each pair disallow the native difference in just its file's one FT coordinate, leaving the other 2,047 intact, and average both donor scores. Native accuracy of 91.76% falls to 82.43%. By defender the changes are 93.43%→81.88% for knights, 95.42%→87.29% for bishops, 96.65%→89.82% for rooks and 86.23%→74.74% for queens. A 40-stratum control selects the eight other White-view channels closest in mean absolute activation difference on one half of the king groups and measures their score effects on the other. The target exceeds all eight in absolute score effect in all 40 strata and in effect per changed activation byte in 38. Even the closest controls often have smaller activation changes, with a median strongest-control/target training ratio of 0.55. [Promotion decisions and random controls](results/promotion-decisions.csv) · [Activity controls](results/activity-controls.csv)

**Played alternatives.** Applying the fixed file-to-channel map to the existing Lichess panels yields 300 informative promotion-dependent comparisons from April–September 2013 and 281 from October–December. One move was played, the other was available from the same parent. Removing the selected FT difference drops accuracy from 96.17% to 89.08% and from 98.22% to 92.35% respectively. These are selected endgame decisions from previously used archives. Playing-strength effects require a separate search experiment. ## Related mechanisms in other endgames

For nonpawn material pairs we sample one strict common-source substitution per eligible king–king–opposing-piece placement, then apply the same Black move deletion to both children in both materials. Both siblings receive the forced-node fallback. All measures below come from legal, check-free starting children and include the native network's errors.

- **Knight fork.** In 2,051 matched rook/queen comparisons, only H permits a Black knight check attacking the white man with the knight safe from immediate capture. Removing these forks erases the order in both materials. With a white rook, keeping H1 units 0, 1 and 4 gives 72.17% against native 72.21%. Allowing just 6/7 to differ gives 53.57%. Removing 1 and 4 gives 57.90%. This identifies sufficient and influential channels on this material and tactic. [Tactical restrictions](results/tactical-controls.csv)
- **Rook skewer.** In 19,032 bishop/queen comparisons Black's order in both materials requires a rook check with the white king between rook and white man. Removing just H2 unit 2's difference sends accuracy from 73.81% to 50.58% with the bishop. On the same geometry with the queen it changes from 71.96% to 71.95%. A stronger test requires a distant rook check after which every legal evasion permits the checking rook to capture the white man immediately. On 8,612 matched pairs, removing H2 unit 2 gives 50.39% against 75.67% native with the bishop, and 75.66% against 75.66% with the queen. H2 indices are local to the second layer. The full per-unit experiment includes outcomes where ablation improves ordering. [Action definitions and forced-capture results](results/tactical-controls.csv) · [Example positions](results/tactical-examples.csv)
- **Rook against rook.** The analogous forced-capture-after-distant-check test yields 5,979 rook/queen substitutions against a black rook. Removing H2 unit 13 sends the white rook's native 89.94% to 74.93%. With the queen the figures are 71.44% and 71.78%. Material-specific use of second-layer signals recurs across two substitution tests. [Full H2 interventions on the broader distant-check set](results/readout-interchange.csv)

## Reproduction

Start with the [completed king and horizon studies](../README.md), the pinned Stockfish 14.1 network, the circuit trace engine and the Syzygy files described in the [atlas](../../../README.md). Run the following on a Linux AVX2 host.

~~~bash
bash research/nnue-tempo/atlas/horizon/kings/routes/run.sh .scratch/nnue
~~~

The independent native trace comparison checks raw sums, clipped FT bytes, H1 logits and bytes, H2 bytes, both score branches on 968 board observations. Separate move generation verifies source eligibility and 96 three-ply escort decisions. The all-rank scanner reproduces the exact earlier 2,830 coordinates. The additional tactical tests use python-chess legality and Syzygy labels. The tensor interchanges evaluate counterfactual activations of this particular network. Claims about the importance of units are conditional on the paired donor convention, material and sampled action. [Native and chess checks](results/native-audit.csv)
